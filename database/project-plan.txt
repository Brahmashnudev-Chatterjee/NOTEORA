Project Plan: Student Notes Marketplace
Market and Competitor Survey
Popular study-notes marketplaces (Docsity, Stuvia, DocMerit, etc.) illustrate key features to emulate. For
example, Docsity boasts over 20 million users worldwide, allowing note-sellers to upload PDF/Word content
and set prices in “points” with no hidden fees or revenue splits【22†L378-L387】. Stuvia (700,000+ sellers)
charges a 30% commission per sale, handling marketing and payouts, so authors keep 70% of revenues
【22†L399-L405】. DocMerit offers up to 85% of each sale to the seller , the highest payout in this niche
【22†L420-L429】. Many platforms use similar models: a free tier for casual uploaders, with paid
subscriptions offering premium benefits (e.g. featured listings, advanced analytics)【27†L319-L328】.
Reviews, star ratings, and subject categorization (by course, level, etc.) are common features. Notes
marketplaces often include search filters (subject, instructor , exam), seller profiles, and secure payment
gateways. Platforms like Payhip go even further , letting users create their own store and keep ~95% of
revenue【24†L486-L495】. 
Key competitor takeaways: 
Buyer experience: Browse notes by subject/course, view details (preview, rating, author info), add to
cart, pay, and download. 
Seller experience: Upload notes (PDF/Word), set price (e.g. Stuvia minimum $2.50), and earn
commission on each sale【22†L399-L405】【24†L486-L495】. Sellers can earn passively once notes
are live. 
Admin features: Review and approve uploads, manage users, track sales, and generate reports.
Quality control is typically enforced (content checks, duplication detection) before notes go live. 
By studying these platforms, the new web app can combine: a clean notes-browsing UX, seller dashboards
for uploads and sales, and robust admin oversight with analytics.
User Workflow and CRUD Operations
Registration & Profile: Users sign up (student, educator , or guest). Profile includes name, contact
info, role (buyer/seller or both). After 5 free uploads, users must subscribe (see Pricing) to upload
more. 
Browse & Search: On login, users see the landing/home page with featured and new notes. They
can filter by subject, level (undergrad, graduate, etc.), price, or instructor . Each note item lists title,
short description, price, seller rating. 
Note Details: Clicking a note shows full description, preview (if allowed), full price (e.g. up to ₹150),
and seller info. Reviews and star-ratings from other users appear here. 
Cart & Purchase: Users add notes to cart. Checkout uses a payment gateway. The system calculates
final price = base price + taxes/fees. After payment confirmation, the PDF is available for download in
the user’s account. Each purchase triggers an update (transaction record) in user history.

Reviews: After purchase, users can rate the note (1–5 stars) and leave comments. Reviews are visible
on the note’s detail page, encouraging quality content. 
Seller Dashboard: Any user can become a seller by uploading notes. In their dashboard, sellers
manage their notes (CRUD on their notes): they can Create (upload up to 5 free PDFs at a time with
title, description, course tags), Read (view existing uploads and sales stats), Update (edit
descriptions or price), or Delete (remove an unpublished note). Upload Approval: New uploads go
to “Pending” and are only visible to buyers after admin approval (moderator must review content to
avoid plagiarism or spam). 
Sales History: Sellers see how many times each note sold, total earnings, and pending payouts.
They can withdraw earnings via integrated payouts (e.g. PayPal/Indian local transfer) once a
minimum balance is reached. 
Overall, the user-side is a typical ecommerce marketplace: accounts, product listings (notes), cart, checkout,
and personal dashboards for buying and selling.
Admin Workflow and Dashboard
Admin Login & Dashboard: A secure admin portal with a dashboard summarizing key metrics (total
users, active sellers, monthly sales revenue, recent uploads, etc.). The dashboard could include
charts of monthly sales and user signups. 
User Management: Admin can view all user accounts (buyers and sellers). Features include
searching/filtering users, viewing profile details, editing or deactivating accounts, and upgrading
accounts (e.g. manually grant Pro/Ultra status). A log of user activity (last login, upload history,
purchase history) is accessible per user . 
Content Management: Admin reviews all uploaded notes. A queue shows “Pending Approval”
items. Admin can approve (make note live) or reject with feedback. Approved notes become
available for sale; rejected notes notify the seller . Admin can also manually edit or remove any note. 
Order & Sales Management: View all transactions and orders: see which notes sold, to whom, and
when. Admin can filter by date or user , and track total revenue. This section also displays commission
collected vs. seller payouts. 
Subscription & Plans: Admin sets pricing for the free tier and subscription plans (Pro ₹199/mo, Ultra
₹499/mo). Admin defines plan benefits (upload limits, commission rate, featured status, etc.) and can
change them over time. They also manage billing (seeing which users are on trial, active subs,
overdue payments). 
Site Configuration: Manage categories/subjects, site-wide announcements or blog posts, FAQs, and
contact/support info. Admin also reviews reported reviews or users (flagged content) and takes
action. 
Analytics & Reporting: The admin panel includes reports like “Total notes uploaded”, “New users
this month”, “Pending approvals”, etc. It may integrate charts for performance. 
In summary, the admin panel is a CRUD hub for all data: Admin can Create/Edit site categories, Read all
records, Update user/note status, and Delete inappropriate content, plus monitor system health. The built-
in Django admin (if using Django) or a custom MERN admin UI can provide these features efficiently
【34†L76-L81】.

Technical Stack Comparison: MERN vs. Django
For the tech stack, we compare MERN (MongoDB, Express, React, Node) versus Django (Python backend)
with React frontend:
Django (with React frontend): Django is a mature Python framework with batteries included. It
provides built‑in authentication, permissions, admin interface, and database migrations out of
the box【34†L76-L81】, which accelerates development of a complex app with users, roles, and
content. Django’s ORM (e.g. with PostgreSQL) suits relational data (users, notes, orders) well. It
enforces structure and security best practices by default. According to experienced developers,
Django+React (separating frontend) yields cleaner , maintainable code for LMS/edtech apps【34†L83-
L90】. The downside is a steeper learning curve and more initial setup. 
MERN: MERN is all-JavaScript: MongoDB as database, Express+Node for server , React for UI. It allows
rapid prototyping (“fun at the start”) since the same language is used everywhere【34†L56-L60】.
However , large apps may require building many features manually: authentication logic, user roles,
security checks all must be coded from scratch【34†L64-L72】. MongoDB’s schemaless nature can
make reporting (e.g. sales analytics) harder than a relational DB. MERN can be faster for an MVP , but
Django’s robustness pays off long-term【34†L114-L120】. 
Performance & Scalability: Both stacks can scale well. Real-world advice is to choose based on team
expertise: “Use MERN for a quick MVP; use Django+React if you want the app to last”【34†L116-
L120】. Django’s built-in admin panel immediately provides an interface for the dashboard needs
(user and content management)【34†L76-L81】. MERN would require building a similar admin UI
from scratch. 
Recommendation: Given the need for user roles, admin dashboards, and content approval workflows,
Django’s out-of-the-box features (auth, admin UI, ORM) make it a strong choice. A React frontend can
provide a modern UI. If fast development and full-JS stack are preferred, MERN is viable, but expect more
custom coding for admin features and security.
Pricing, Commission, and Subscriptions
Note Pricing & Commission: By design, each PDF note can be sold for up to ₹150. We must
incorporate platform commission and taxes. For example, if a note sells at ₹150, an 18% GST (per
Indian tax law on digital goods) adds ₹27, making the customer’s total ₹177【20†L48-L56】. If we
follow a marketplace commission of 30% (as Stuvia does【22†L399-L405】), the platform keeps ₹45
of the base ₹150, and the seller receives ₹105 before tax. The seller is responsible for the ₹27 GST
(collected from buyer) and remits it to the government, so their net earnings from that sale are ₹78.
The platform’s profit is the ₹45 commission (before any applicable tax on its earnings). A simple
breakdown: 
Sale Price: ₹150 (base) + ₹27 (GST) = ₹177 total paid. 
Platform Commission: ₹45 (30% of ₹150). 
Seller Revenue (pre-tax): ₹150 – ₹45 = ₹105. Seller then pays ₹27 GST, netting ₹78.
These numbers can be adjusted: for example, if the platform charges a flat 20% commission, seller
keeps ₹120, platform ₹30. Any chosen commission (15–30%) should be clearly stated in site terms.
(Note: Docsity promises no hidden fees【22†L378-L387】, so you may opt for a very transparent
split.)

Portal Profit: The portal’s profit per sale is its commission (here ₹45). On ₹150 base, platform would
record ₹45 revenue plus handle the ₹27 GST (passing it to tax authorities). Over many sales, monthly
commission revenue can be tracked on the admin dashboard. 
Subscription Plans: To encourage loyalty and monetize active sellers, offer tiered subscriptions after
5 free uploads. For example: 
Free (Basic): Up to 5 uploads. Standard 30% commission. Limited support. 
Pro (₹199/month): Allow ~15 uploads/month, reduce commission to, say, 20%. Priority listing
(seller’s notes get featured), basic analytics of sales. Possibly 24-hour admin support. 
Ultra (₹499/month): Unlimited uploads, lowest commission (e.g. 10%), premium features like deep
analytics (download stats, demographic insights), and marketing boosts (featured in newsletters or
on homepage). Also maybe free content review (fast-track approvals). 
Subscription models are common in edu-marketplaces【27†L319-L328】. At ₹199, Pro sellers get a tangible
perk (e.g. more sales percentage and exposure). At ₹499, Ultra sellers get full control and extra services.
These benefits should be explicitly detailed on the pricing page (e.g. “Pro: +10 uploads, 20% fee; Ultra:
unlimited uploads, 10% fee, plus featured seller status”). Include Indian currency symbols (₹) in the UI for
clarity. 
Additional Fees: If external payment gateways charge transaction fees, these should be factored in
(deducted from either seller payout or portal expense as per policy). Clearly communicate any GST
handling: typically, sellers as service providers would be responsible for GST collection/remittance
since both parties are in India【20†L48-L56】.
Site Architecture and Pages
Landing (Public) Page: A modern, responsive homepage in cream, black, and orange tones (per request)
with slide-out cards or a carousel highlighting top subjects and notes. Key sections include: 
Hero banner (“Buy & Sell Study Notes Easily – For Free!”) with CTA buttons (Sign Up, Browse Notes). 
How It Works (3 steps: Upload Notes, Approve & List, Students Buy). 
Featured Categories or Subjects (cards linking to search results). 
Testimonials (quote from a student/instructor). 
Pricing (free vs. Pro vs. Ultra with benefits summary). 
FAQ (e.g. payment, security, support). 
Footer (contact, terms, social links). 
User Pages:
Signup/Login: Standard forms with email (OTP/verification) or social login. CAPTCHA to avoid bots. 
Dashboard (after login): Personalized. 
My Purchases: List of bought notes with download links and revisit rating option. 
My Uploads: List of notes uploaded by user , showing status (Pending, Live, Rejected) and stats
(views/sales). Buttons to upload new note (form with title, course tags, price, PDF upload). After 5
uploads, the button directs to subscription page. 
Account Settings: Edit profile, payment methods, change password.

Visual Style: Use cream as a neutral background, black for text/headers, and orange highlights (buttons,
icons). Implement smooth animations and slide cards on homepage (e.g. each subject card slides in). Use
modern UI components (drop shadows, rounded corners). Examples of similar designs can be seen in
educational apps (though none were directly cited, they follow standard SaaS marketplace patterns).
In summary, the app will combine e-commerce and community features: public landing/marketing pages,
rich user dashboards (browse, buy, sell), and robust admin panels. All pages should maintain the stated
color scheme and clean, responsive design.
